# Stone for groups 7 and 8

The group 1 stone note was unavailable when painting began. These choices are provisional until the coordinator matches the sets.

Ancient worked stone is warm honey limestone: cream lit faces, pale ochre bevels, muted mauve shadows. Use large wedge stones around round arches, broad worn edges, a diamond keystone and occasional shallow leaf cuts. Keep the surface matte. Gold belongs to applied metal and Calla's seal, rather than the stone itself. The Lamp Gallery's brass lamps are dead; their pale lenses do not emit light.

Natural exposed rock is lavender-grey with warm cream sunlit planes. Use broad, rounded, asymmetric masses and sparse cracks. Moss collects on upper faces and sheltered seams in olive and yellow-green patches. Rock arches have continuous eroded stone, without masonry joints.

Oak roots and bark use dark umber recesses, brown middle tones and ochre lit ribs. Living roots follow a few broad curves; small foliage and bark marks can be painted rather than modelled. Crystals are milk-white with lilac shadows and a faint cyan tint, without neon emission.

The image palette takes precedence over these approximate descriptions. Dimensions in the plan take precedence over incidental perspective distortion in a painting.
